# Storyboard — French Museums: From Crisis to Recovery (2014–2024)

## Our question and audience

**How did the COVID-19 pandemic affect museum attendance in France, and how did attendance recover between 2021 and 2024?**

This dashboard is for students and people interested in museums and cultural activity. It follows a simple story: before the crisis, during the crisis and after the crisis.

## 1. The Big Picture — The COVID Shock

**Visual:** three KPIs for 2019, 2020 and 2024.

We start with the main change. Recorded admissions fall from **65,933,388 in 2019** to **23,819,133 in 2020**, then reach **71,192,245 in 2024**. These numbers quickly show the shock and the later recovery. The number of reporting museums changes between years.

**Transition:** Did attendance recover immediately, or did it take several years?

## 2. Attendance Evolution — Understanding the Recovery

**Visual:** an interactive line chart from 2014 to 2024.

Before Covid, attendance changes from year to year. In 2020, it falls sharply during lockdowns and museum closures. Visits start to return in 2021 and rise strongly in 2022, but remain below 2019. The recorded total passes 2019 in 2023 and rises more slowly in 2024. Tourism and changing habits could help explain this path.

**Transition:** Does this overall recovery mean that every region recovered in the same way?

## 3. Regional Recovery — Not All Regions Recovered Equally

**Visuals:** a regional bar chart and an interactive map showing percentage change from 2019 to 2024.

We compare the same **990 sites** at both dates to make the comparison fairer. Their combined attendance increases by **7.61%**, but **9 of 19** regional groups remain below 2019. Île-de-France has more admissions, while Pays de la Loire has faster percentage growth. Percentages compare each region with its own starting level.

Local closures and renovations help us understand some large falls in Mayotte, Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon and Guyane. A territory with only one museum in the comparison is strongly affected by that museum's situation. Recorded zeros stay in the analysis.

**Transition:** Did paid and free admissions follow the same path?

## 4. Paid vs Free Admissions — Did the Recovery Change?

**Visual:** a grouped bar chart for the same **814 sites** with complete and consistent records from 2019 to 2024.

Both types fall during the crisis, but free admissions fall less compared with 2019. The gap becomes smaller in 2020 and 2021. Both rise in 2022 and 2023. In 2024, paid admissions rise while free admissions fall. The smaller crisis-period gap seems mainly temporary. Tourism and household budgets are possible explanations, not proven causes.

## 5. What Did We Learn? — Key Findings

**Presentation:** a short green takeaway followed by the main conclusions.

Overall, museum attendance recovered, with a modest increase for the same 990 sites. Recovery differs between territories. Local situations also matter. Next, we could study tourism, local resources and museum opening periods to better understand these differences.

## 6. Explore Museum Attendance

**Interaction:** change the region, year range or attendance type in the sidebar. The three exploration KPIs, line chart, bar chart and map update. Hover shows more details. These controls do not change the fixed story above.

This lets the reader explore a region or period after understanding the national story.

## 7. Data Quality and Limitations

**Presentation:** two short text sections.

We explain the cleaning: **12,292 original rows**, **4 unclear rows excluded**, **3 missing total counts** and **19 payment inconsistencies**. We do not invent missing data or remove recorded zeros. The data is yearly and coverage changes. It cannot show visitor motives or prove the exact effect of Covid.

**Final message:** museums generally recovered, but the national result does not describe every museum or territory.
